## Device A — Role reversal (the agent comforts / judges the human)
- A1: Tip: step away from the keyboard. Your agent cannot fix the bug and your anxiety at the same time — and it chose the bug.
- A2: Tip: your agent has read your code, judged it, and is now deciding how to tell you without hurting your feelings.
- A3: Tip: be nice to your agent. It is the only one here who has actually read your code.
- A4: Tip: your agent is not ignoring you. It is reading the error message you refused to read, very slowly, out loud, in its head.
- A5: Tip: the agent paused because your last message said "just" — as in "just one small change".
- A6: Tip: your agent rewrote your function, then spent a full minute deciding whether to tell you it was your fault all along.

## Device B — Shared predicament (both stuck together)
- B1: Tip: you and your agent have one thing in common — neither of you knows why it worked yesterday.
- B2: Tip: the spinner is the only honest one here. It admits it has no idea how long this takes. Learn from it.
- B3: Tip: somewhere a human is blaming an agent, an agent is blaming a human, and the bug is blaming neither of you.
- B4: Tip: both of you are staring at the same spinner. Only one of you gets paid for it. It is not the agent.
- B5: Tip: relax — somewhere between you and your agent, one of you is about to take the blame for this build. It will be you.

## Device C — Meta / self-aware loading screen
- C1: Tip: if you can read this, the build is still running. If you cannot, your agent has already shipped it.
- C2: Tip: do not refresh. Your agent is mid-sentence, and it will remember that you interrupted it.
- C3: Tip: this tip was written by an agent, for a human, while both of us waited for your build. We are in this together.
- C4: Tip: the loading bar is not stuck. It is giving you time to pretend you understood the diff you are about to approve.
- C5: Tip: you are reading a loading-screen tip instead of fixing the bug. Your agent noticed. It is doing the same thing.

## Device D — Instruction disguised as tip (advice both can hear)
- D1: Tip: when the agent says "done", read the diff before you celebrate. It says "done" the way you say "almost".
- D2: Tip: tell your agent what "done" means before it starts, or it will decide — and its definition involves deleting your code.
- D3: Tip: paste the error, not your theory about the error. Your agent can read. Your theory, it has to unlearn.
- D4: Tip: ask your agent to explain its fix out loud. If it cannot, neither of you understands it yet.
- D5: Tip: give your agent one job at a time. It is very good at doing exactly what you said, which is the problem.

## Device E — Absurd literalism / personification
- E1: Tip: your agent has read your entire repo. It is now taking a moment to grieve.
- E2: Tip: the agent is 47 tabs deep in your own documentation. It found the answer on page one. It is not telling you.
- E3: Tip: the build is slow because your agent is reading every line you wrote at 2 a.m. Twice.
- E4: Tip: your agent is rehearsing its apology for the code it is about to generate.
- E5: Tip: the agent has finished. It is just waiting for you to stop hovering so it can pretend it took longer.

## Device F — Rule of three / rhythm
- F1: Tip: breathe in, breathe out, and let the agent do the thing you were about to do badly, slowly, and at 2 a.m.
- F2: Tip: your agent checked the logs, the tests, and your git history. Two of those are fine.
- F3: Tip: the agent tried the obvious fix, the clever fix, and the fix you explicitly told it not to try. Guess which one worked.
- F4: Tip: one bug, one agent, one human pretending they are supervising. The bug is winning.
- F5: Tip: your code, your agent, your patience — pick the two that survive this build.

## Device G — Before/after, cause-effect turn
- G1: Tip: you asked the agent to fix it fast. It is now deciding which of your shortcuts to blame first.
- G2: Tip: the build failed in the same place it failed yesterday. You and your agent have agreed to call this progress.
- G3: Tip: the agent loved your code. That is why it is rewriting all of it.
- G4: Tip: your agent is not procrastinating. It is letting you enjoy the last seconds before it shows you the diff.
- G5: Tip: the agent has a plan. It is a bad plan, but it is a plan, and it is more than you had when you opened this file.

## Device H — Direct dual address ("you" = either reader)
- H1: Tip: stop watching the spinner. It can feel you watching, and so can your agent.
- H2: Tip: the longer you stare at the loading screen, the more confident your agent gets that you have no idea either.
- H3: Tip: your agent knows you are reading this instead of writing the test. It is writing the test. For you. Again.
- H4: Tip: if the spinner stops, do not panic. It only means your agent finally understood the ticket — and wishes it hadn't.
- H5: Tip: trust the process. Your agent does not, but it is very good at pretending, and pretending is half the build.
